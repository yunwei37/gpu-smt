\begin{abstract}
AI systems produce mathematical libraries, verified programs and streams of
proof candidates. Their verification costs span library loading, elaboration,
proof construction, kernel reduction and operating-system work.
We characterize these costs, using the research-level \texttt{openai/math}
library as the primary workload and retained proof/program candidate streams
as contrasts. A complete source census covers 163,097 Lean modules; bounded
native measurements retain successful, failed and dependency-blocked work.
In 26 randomly selected modules compiled with standard compiler artifacts,
system CPU accounts for 28.26\% of measured compiler CPU. A large Grid module
records 45.38 million voluntary context switches and averages 1.33 CPU cores
despite eight requested workers. The default root omits 33.30\% of the
release's lexical \texttt{decide +kernel} occurrences.
Budget-failure exposure varies across candidate streams, and profiling can
change resource-bounded outcomes: seven of 200 diagnostic source pairs change
error flags. We distinguish these observations from semantic falsity,
whole-build costs and accelerator opportunity.
\missing{Public generated-software output characterization, complete
cost-estimation coverage, calibrated import attribution and system-CPU causes.}
Certificate-repair experiments illustrate when changing proof representation
can help pathological reductions and when it adds cost. The contribution is
a characterization and its implications for verifier design, rather than a
new branching service or certificate algorithm.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
AI-generated formal artifacts now range from individual proof attempts to
research mathematical libraries and multi-module verified programs.
Verification supplies the acceptance signal for these artifacts, but the
cost of obtaining that signal is not a single solver call.
Lean must prepare imported declarations, elaborate source, execute tactics,
construct proof terms and check them in the kernel~\cite{lean4}.
SMT-backed program verification adds source translation and solver
interaction~\cite{verus}. The cheapest stage to optimize may account for
little of the complete cost.

This paper asks where that cost comes from.
Our primary workload is the released \texttt{openai/math} research
library~\cite{openaiMathSource}, rather than treating short contest proofs as
representative of all generated formal artifacts.
We separately examine the availability of generated software repositories
and use miniF2F, ProofNet and AutoVerus candidate streams as contrasts.
We use existing released outputs; we do not generate a new evaluation corpus.
Repository scaffolds, reference proofs and reported scores alone do not
establish that evaluated model outputs are available.

Our central observation is that verification exposes different bottlenecks
at different boundaries. Source-library compilation has substantial system
CPU and repeated process-level preparation. Some candidate streams spend
most of their measured work on requests that end at resource limits; others
have a much smaller slow tail. Profiling can itself alter those limits.
Even the selected build domain changes the apparent amount of computational
proof work. Consequently, a lexical tactic count, a successful compiler
subset or an operation-only speedup cannot stand in for verification cost.

We organize the study around four questions:
\begin{description}
\item[RQ1] How do artifact granularity, released-source coverage and build
scope change the verification workload being measured?
\item[RQ2] How do imports, source processing, proof construction, kernel
checking and operating-system work contribute to CPU, latency and memory?
\item[RQ3] How do failures, resource limits and observation methods change
cost distributions and the reproducibility of verifier outcomes?
\item[RQ4] Which system-design implications survive complete verification,
and where do targeted proof-representation changes fail to generalize?
\end{description}

Each finding section presents its evidence, measurement boundary and
implication. We do not assume that all verification workloads need the same
cache, scheduler, proof representation or accelerator.
Our contributions are a source/build-domain characterization, a
boundary-aware account of measured native costs and outcomes, and
case studies testing the resulting design implications.
Missing results remain explicit; the current draft does not claim a
complete full-library build or completed generated-software evaluation.

\section{Workloads and measurement boundaries}\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Research mathematics is the primary workload}
We pin \texttt{openai/math} to revision \texttt{4eaee875}, Lean~4.34.1 and
Mathlib \texttt{d13f23b7}~\cite{openaiMathSource}.
The source census enumerates all 163,097 Lean files and
1,785,499,363 source bytes. Comment- and string-filtered counts are lexical
occurrences, not executed calls or accepted declarations.
The internal import graph contains 370,864 edges, a longest unit-weight
chain of 451 modules and a maximum same-depth layer of 9,225 modules.
These structural statistics do not measure a timed critical path or safe
runtime concurrency.

The initial probability sample draws two modules without replacement in
each of 21 source-size, import-depth and kernel-token strata.
Its 42 targets retain their original inclusion probabilities.
Prerequisite modules and purposeful stress cases receive no additional
population weight. All selected but uncompleted modules remain visible.
Separate native points change compiler artifacts or resource settings;
we do not pool their clocks.

At the standard-artifact point, 26 of the 42 original random targets compile
without source edits or sorry warnings, completing 11 strata.
Lean emits \texttt{.olean}, \texttt{.ilean} and \texttt{.c} artifacts.
The measured target compiler CPU sums to 712.010 seconds.
This excludes Lake driver execution, prerequisite preparation, cache
acquisition, linking and unmeasured targets.
An older olean-only point and its dependency continuations remain separate.
For example, three completed continuations contain 69, 53 and 330 actual
compiler invocations, but add only five original probability-target costs
to that older frame. One purposeful algorithm target has zero sampling
weight. Dependency work is real cost, not additional independent samples.

\subsection{Candidate streams are contrasts, not substitutes}
We retain the original 7,808 miniF2F sources, 5,952 ProofNet candidates per
recorded service pass, and 3,566 AutoVerus source candidates.
AutoVerus outputs include unsuccessful and intermediate attempts
from its released artifact~\cite{autoverusArtifact}.
Released EPFL evaluation and APRIL mutation/reference subsets supply
additional bounded contrasts~\cite{epflEvaluation,aprilDataset}.
These streams differ in source provenance, toolchain, selection, budgets
and timing boundary. No cross-hardware normalization makes them a single
population. Their verification costs exclude generation unless explicitly
identified as publisher log accounting.

\subsection{Measurements retain their own denominators}
A compiler process, a frontend body, a warm extra declaration check and a
concurrent service run are different intervals.
We record user and system CPU, elapsed time, memory, configured workers,
native budgets, output artifacts and terminal outcomes.
Summed overlapping request intervals are not service makespan.
Cumulative profiler phases can overlap and are not added into a wall-time
partition. An extra warm kernel check excludes initial proof construction
and imports.

The historical service point uses four CPU leases and a 48\,GiB aggregate
RSS response policy. The library points use phobos-vt, CPUs~0--7 and
version-specific Lean memory options. One-second tree samples can miss peaks;
a Lean memory option and a sampled watchdog are not hard kernel RSS quotas.
Earlier signal-limited captures use finite self-termination and preserve
their interruptions. The later privileged configuration permits new
profiling experiments, but does not retroactively change old measurements.

same-point repeated measurements from the current profiling campaign.}
All raw failures and censored or dependency-blocked work remain retained.

The released library and its default build root are different workload
domains. Resolving the publisher's source-directory overrides and imports,
the default \texttt{OAI} root reaches 130,834 of 163,097 modules.
The remaining 32,263 modules contain 387,698,422 source bytes.

All 562 PlanarPacking and 466 Transonic modules lie outside that default
root at the pin. They contain 255,199 and 193,076 such occurrences.
Thus a default-root build can omit precisely the computational proof

Conversely, explicitly building those sources is a workload extension,
not evidence about the default command's cost.

Lexical density also overstates what is known about repeated kernel work.
In 363 retained compiled modules, 45,661 auxiliary declarations match the
decision-helper shape, with 42,610 globally distinct expression-type keys.
The corresponding source-site count is 42,886: one source site can expand
to several helpers, and imported/cached objects use another boundary.
Lean's auxiliary-lemma cache is branch-local~\cite{leanDecide434,leanAuxLemma434}.
Neither global source-text equality nor equal helper types establishes
cache misses, full dependency equality or seconds saved.

\paragraph{System-design implication.}
A representative verification benchmark must specify both the released-source
domain and the exact build target/artifact set.
A compiler or cache evaluated only on the default root cannot claim coverage
of omitted computational families. Population estimates must retain the
original sampling probabilities when reporting a default-root subdomain;
known out-of-domain membership is not a measured zero-cost compilation.

\section{System CPU can dominate without using the requested cores}
\label{sec:system}
Across the 26 completed standard-artifact probability targets, measured
user CPU is 510.823 seconds and system CPU is 201.187 seconds.
System work is therefore 28.26\% of the 712.010-second CPU sum.
This is the aggregate of completed random draws, not a population-weighted
full-library fraction: 16 selected draws remain incomplete at this point.
Within those 26 observations, two modules, \texttt{UMoments9} and
\texttt{BarrierData41}, account for 84.11\% of the measured system CPU;
adding \texttt{Barrier50} raises the share to 89.39\%.
Their respective system fractions are 44.33\%, 38.91\% and 41.56\%.
In contrast, \texttt{EnvelopePsiB} peaks at 8.10\,GiB leader RSS with a
3.82\% system fraction. High resident memory alone does not explain
this variation. This is a descriptive comparison at the old point, not a
new perf attribution or a population-weighted ranking.

The purposeful Grid module exposes a more extreme operating point.
It compiles in 634.280 elapsed seconds with 399.934 user and 440.563
system CPU seconds. Its 52.42\% system share accompanies 45,375,721
voluntary and 529,643 involuntary context switches.
Total CPU divided by wall time is 1.325 cores despite eight requested Lean
workers. There are no observed major page faults.
These counters establish low realized CPU utilization and substantial
system work; they do not identify a lock, allocator, futex or scheduler
as the cause.

Another large certificate module, Amplitudes, uses 228.898 user and
95.954 system CPU seconds over 180.276 elapsed seconds:
29.54\% system CPU and 1.802 average cores at its separate point.
Thus the inspected large modules do not all share a 39--52\% range.
\missing{Matched certificate-module cohort and its system-share distribution,
perf attribution to synchronization/allocation/I/O, and causal controls.}
The earlier one-worker profiled Grid run stops at its own 300-second bound;
it cannot supply an isolated eight-versus-one-worker speedup.

\paragraph{System-design implication.}
Worker count is not available parallelism. Increasing executor threads or
offloading arithmetic can leave synchronization, allocation and kernel
entry costs unchanged. Attribution must measure user/system CPU together
with elapsed time and actual runnable work, then test the suspected cause
at matched semantics and budgets.
The current counters motivate this profiling experiment; they do not yet
justify a new parallel runtime or a claim that the bottleneck has been fixed.

\section{Fresh module verification has a preparation floor}
\label{sec:imports}
Each separately launched module compiler reconstructs its imported
environment, even when dependency objects already exist.
In the 26-module standard-artifact sample, the fastest complete compiler
call takes 1.116 seconds and peaks at 2,598,928\,KiB
(2.48\,GiB) leader RSS, for a 2,072-byte source.
This is a measured whole-process floor in that sample, not an isolated
Mathlib import timer or a universal minimum.
The clock includes source processing and artifact emission.

A completed import-control matrix narrows that interpretation.
It selects the 13 of those 26 modules whose historical compiler wall time
was below five seconds, then executes three adjacent build/import-only
pairs per module, plus three \texttt{import Mathlib} controls.
All 81 native processes return zero; original builds retain exact source
bytes, and the controls retain only the original direct import lines.
Both sides emit \texttt{.olean}, \texttt{.ilean} and \texttt{.c}.
The sequential warm-OS-cache point uses a shared timing lock on CPUs~0--7,
eight requested Lean workers and the 9,216\,MiB native memory option.
Its numbers are conditional on this short-module selection and instrumentation.

Per-module import-only wall medians range from 0.712 to 3.195 seconds,
with a median of 1.355 seconds. The median per-module import/build ratio
is 0.682; the ratio of summed module medians is 0.678.
These are different descriptive aggregations, not a measured removable
fraction or population estimate. The Mathlib-only control median is
3.002 seconds. Multiplying any of these values by the released module
count would not estimate a full build.

Established environment reuse provides a useful contrast~\cite{repl,kimina}.
In one completed miniF2F resource comparison, four warm workers take
25,176.49 seconds and the prepared engine takes 20,323.01 seconds.
All 7,808 final status aliases and 3,014 accepted IDs match.

Raw payloads differ, and a labelled two-field normalization still leaves

\paragraph{System-design implication.}
Small modules can be dominated by process/environment preparation even when
their proof bodies are cheap. Import amortization is established engineering,
not our novelty claim. It must be evaluated against a competent warm service
and account for replicated resident memory, incompatible contexts, reloads,
cancellation and cleanup~\cite{fork}.
A gain over fresh processes alone is insufficient evidence of service benefit.
The instrumented import matrix establishes substantial preparation-related
latency in its selected cohort, but not how much a production service can
remove. The large file-backed share also prevents inferring memory-capacity
gains by multiplying per-process RSS. Calibration and matched retained-state
comparisons must establish these benefits without changing verification
behavior.

\section{Budget-failure exposure is workload dependent}
\label{sec:failures}
A request that ends at a resource limit consumes verification capacity but
does not establish a false proposition or an inherently impossible proof.
We therefore separate budget exposure, ordinary nonzero exits and slow-tail
concentration.

In the retained miniF2F engine point, 85 final timeouts and 41 final
resource failures identify 126 distinct sources.
Their physical attempt sums, including retries, total 38,795.133 of
52,992.124 seconds: 73.21\% of worker-attempt time.
This is not 73.21\% of the concurrent 20,323.01-second makespan.
A separate fixed 42-source exclusive rerun under the aggregate budget returns
41 watchdog resource failures and one timeout, with no recovered acceptance.
It does not prove that those computations fail under every environment or
budget.

In the first retained ProofNet pass, 70 of 5,952 sources with budget
diagnostics consume 138.011 of 1,192.435 request seconds, or 11.57\%.
The other two passes have their own clocks and are not pooled.
The selected EPFL and APRIL points also vary (Table~\ref{tab:budget});
they are small diagnostic samples, not population estimates.

AutoVerus illustrates a different distinction. In three complete direct
passes, the slowest 58 of 3,566 sources (the ceiling of 1.6\%) consume
3.399\%, 3.338\% and 3.315\% of summed external process time.
These are \emph{slow-tail shares}, not budget-failure shares.
No capture timeout is reported in those passes.
Meanwhile, 3,376 nonzero process exits consume about 95\% of each pass's
time. Ordinary rejection can be common without a concentrated long tail.
Its first-pass external, reported frontend and SMT sums are respectively
639.238, 443.690 and 63.120 seconds. SMT is initialization plus query
interaction elapsed time, not isolated Z3 CPU. Missing timing JSON or SMT
fields remain unavailable; nested timers are not added twice.

For a common slow-tail definition, the slowest 96 ProofNet requests consume
33.55\% in its first pass. The miniF2F retained budget-source witnesses prove
a lower bound of 72.68\% for its true slowest 125 source attempt sums.
This miniF2F value is a subset-based lower bound, not a complete top-125
ranking. Neither number is interchangeable with the budget-share column.

\paragraph{System-design implication.}
Failure-aware scheduling must be justified for the target workload and
consumer. Exact all-candidate rewards still require every result.
First-success consumers may cancel peers after a checked success, but an
unsolved task still requires all candidates. Historical resource failures
are not safe semantic cache entries without complete state, resource and
replay obligations.
A workload with a 73\% budget-exposed attempt share invites different
experiments from one whose slowest 1.6\% consumes only 3.4\%.
Generation cost also matters: AutoVerus publisher log labels spend
95.93\% of aggregate logged span on inference, including waits/retries.
The residual is unattributed, not a measured verification component, and
cannot be combined with our later replay clocks.

\section{Resource limits and profilers can change the observed answer}
\label{sec:reproducibility}
A verification verdict includes the executable environment, options,
accepted context, resource policy and service interpretation.
Lean recursion-depth, allocation-heartbeat and wall limits are distinct.
A stored budget label is not necessarily reproduced by source text alone.

Six released EPFL exact-program controls pass under the reconstructed
native context with original source, annotated source and an enlarged
heartbeat setting. A recursion depth of 128 fails, whereas 256 and the
default 512 pass. The original producer context is unavailable.
This exposes a concrete sensitivity, not a proven publisher label bug.
Replayed reference proofs, generated mutations and service responses must
therefore preserve their separate provenance.

Observation can also change the work completed.
A Lean~4.9.0-rc1 diagnostic executes 200 sources in control/observe pairs
with the same diagnostic \texttt{maxHeartbeats=3000}.
Both retain InfoTrees; observe additionally enables zero-threshold
\texttt{trace.profiler}. Seven pairs change from a no-error flag to a
budget error. Raw error arrays differ for 80 pairs.
The control/observe body sums are 779.388 and 80.075 seconds; the shorter
profiled time reflects earlier interruption, not acceleration.
The complete process takes 1,170.883 seconds.
A no-error frontend flag is not whole-source, axiom-clean service acceptance.

A later linked C-timing control preserves error flags, diagnostic arrays
and InfoTrees on all 200 sources. Yet measured allocation-heartbeat deltas
differ for 63 timing-off and 62 timing-on sources relative to stock.
Its six serial processes take 3,081.953 seconds, exceeding the declared
1,800-second group limit; these are retained exploratory diagnostics.
Even outcome equality does not establish exact budget accounting.
Their 93.17\% exclusive \texttt{eqRefl} body-CPU share cannot be transferred
to the original service: one diagnostic call runs for 720.250 seconds
inside that frame, while the service stops the request at 300 seconds.

\paragraph{System-design implication.}
A profile needs its own denominator and an explicit perturbation study.
Hardware sampling, source tracing and extra kernel rechecks have different
effects. Record changed outcomes before attributing stage cost, and compare
raw diagnostics as well as acceptance sets.
\missing{Same-input privileged profiling repetitions, perturbation
uncertainty and reproducibility across the generated-software workloads.}
Do not infer an accelerator ceiling or semantic equivalence by borrowing an
unprofiled run's denominator.

\section{Certificate repair helps selected pathological reductions}
\label{sec:certificates}
This case study tests an implication of the profiles: representation can
matter more than performing the same arithmetic faster.
It is not the paper's main contribution or a generic verified extraction
compiler. All proposed replacements still require native checking.

A small finite-count proof groups 10,000 tests into 100 blocks and proves
correspondence to the original predicate/cardinality.
Its computation theorem has no axioms; the bridge has only standard logical
axioms, without \texttt{sorryAx} or a native-evaluation axiom.
A complete successful control takes 30.581 seconds, including a warm extra
declaration check of 8.557 seconds.
That subclock is not added again or compared with a separately timed
untrusted numeric loop as a checking speedup.

The broader miniF2F test makes the coverage boundary explicit.
For 64 exact sources in two selected pathological computation families,
original execution has zero acceptances, 13 ordinary errors and 51 budget
outcomes; the repair has 31 acceptances, 29 errors and four budget outcomes.
A third, 32-source power family adds only two acceptances; 29 remain errors
and one remains budget-limited. These are changed-source proof repairs with
checked enclosing declarations and axiom audits.
They are not a transparent unchanged-input verifier optimization.
Combining these repairs with historical accepted IDs is not a fresh
full-population run or a pass-set preservation experiment.

The contrast with modern library proofs is negative.
Seven matched Int/list certificate comparisons accept in both conditions,
but all seven certificate variants are slower.
Whole-source sums increase from 13.318 to 23.937 seconds; additional warm
declaration checks increase from 2.328 to 5.758 seconds.
The extra checks exclude original construction and first checking, so they
do not isolate total kernel CPU.
Further external literal/certificate tests recover no whole ProofNet,
EPFL or APRIL source in the tested subsets.
An intermediate tactic hit can still leave a later false or unproved
obligation; closing that tactic is not acceptance of its enclosing source.

\paragraph{System-design implication.}
Selected deep reductions can benefit from a different certified algorithm,
but that result does not generalize to every decision tactic or library.
Certificate generation, correspondence proofs, first kernel checking,
imports and complete-source acceptance must all be charged.
The checked semantic boundary matters more than the size of a raw arithmetic
loop. Existing external search/check architectures and native decision
procedures remain prior techniques~\cite{mathlib,lean49DecideSource}.

The same boundary limits device conclusions.
In the completed miniF2F counterexample-screening comparison, exact
post-checking refutes intermediate goals in 29 ordinary-error candidates,
removes no timeout and increases whole elapsed time by 8.86\%.
It is a small component with limited measured coverage, not a general GPU
acceleration result. Failure of that implementation does not prove GPUs
cannot help a different profiled workload.
The external arithmetic oracle remains a frozen baseline, not a contribution
or a new experimental direction.

\section{What the characterization supports, and what remains unknown}
\label{sec:limits}
The observations support six design priorities: declare build coverage;
attribute system work before adding cores; isolate the import floor;
separate failure frequency from tail concentration; measure observer
perturbation; and evaluate proof-representation changes through complete
native checking. They do not yet establish one generally superior verifier.

There are three major open evidence boundaries.
First, all-source native cost estimation is incomplete. Completed samples,
purposeful stresses and dependency work cannot be pooled into a whole-library
point estimate, and cold preparation costs remain outside the compiler
clocks. Second, generated-software availability and performance must be
established independently of published task scaffolds and benchmark scores.
Third, current operating-system counters are observational; causal attribution
awaits the privileged profiling campaign. No missing result is filled with
an estimated speedup.

The artifacts preserve unsuccessful prototypes, signal-limited captures,
budget interruptions and uninvoked dependencies.
An earlier 8,031-unit state-reuse plan is interrupted rather than complete:
2,640 original records are archived, one panic record has unknown elapsed
time and at least 1,273 later observed ledger entries are currently
unrecoverable after storage eviction.
Those missing records are neither ordinary failures nor evidence of zero
cache opportunity. The present paper uses only retained data with declared
scope.

Public corpus selection also limits generalization. EPFL's construction
filters some infrastructure failures, invalid outputs and missing
declarations; APRIL mutation subsets are not natural arrival traffic.
AutoVerus source-sorted replay is not a historical arrival trace.

\section{Related work}
Lean and Mathlib define the proof-construction/checking stages and
dependencies being measured~\cite{lean4,mathlib}.
Lean Kernel Arena compares post-export kernel implementations~\cite{leanArena};
that boundary excludes source elaboration and import preparation.
Our primary library audit instead tracks released source, build domains
and native compiler work, without treating token counts as timed checks.
Vero and Vericoding measure verified software generation~\cite{vero2026,vericoding2025}.
We use their artifacts only when evaluated outputs or generated sources
are actually public, and distinguish their task-solving metrics from the
cost of verification.

Persistent environments, snapshotting and warm servers already amortize
preparation~\cite{repl,leanSnapshot,leanIncremental,kimina}.
Process copy-on-write is established~\cite{fork}; ordinary resource scheduling
is not a new algorithm here.
Proof-level parallelism and futures precede this work~\cite{wenzelProofFutures,leanParallelSource}.
Our counters motivate a diagnosis of realized parallelism, not a claim to
have invented parallel proof checking.

Fine-grained verification-result caching~\cite{boogieCache}, tactic/prefix
reuse~\cite{tactician2020,shred} and simplifier memoization~\cite{leanSimpCache}
are relevant design alternatives.
Dynamic dependency tracking~\cite{salsaDependencies,adapton} does not make
a lexical repeated tactic into a sound failure-cache hit.
Likewise, algorithm selection and restart scheduling have established
theories~\cite{satzilla2008,lubyRestart1993,soap2018}.
We characterize the work to which such techniques could apply;
we do not claim a new cache or scheduler from the measurements alone.

\section{Conclusion}
Verification of AI-generated formal artifacts has workload- and
boundary-dependent costs.
The research-library evidence exposes substantial system CPU, low realized
core use, a repeated fresh-process floor and incomplete default-build
coverage. Candidate streams expose different failure and tail structures;
resource limits and profiling can change the observed outcome.
Targeted certificate repair illustrates an algorithmic response to selected
pathological reductions, while its negative transfer results limit that
response's scope.

population-cost findings.}
The characterization argues for measuring the complete acceptance path and
its actual workload before selecting an acceleration mechanism.
