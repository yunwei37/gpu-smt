Independence is grounded in native declaration-checking architecture, rather than speculative argument readiness. New device workers may lose sharing, duplicate native checks, suffer skew, or spend more on typed packing and scratch than they save. Whole-declaration batching, closure conversion, integer IDs and resident contexts are established mechanisms; absence of an Arena GPU entry is not a novelty argument. A useful result needs actual expensive work moved and a full gain over the optimized CPU, with a causal account of these costs.

The best alternative remains discovering a differentiated deeper native preparation/service opportunity. Existing single-header evidence and closest work weaken another import-only/cache/fork experiment, and no measured surviving deeper-prefix mechanism currently dominates E036. Thus the cost-first complete-declaration proposal is a defensible next use of effort. This assessment does not prove it beats every uninvestigated system design or authorize an unrelated CPU tuning grid.

A positive complete result could provide consequential device-checking evidence and motivate a supported integration; a negative measured opportunity or complete loss can close this constructor; mixed results bound workload/support/skew; incomplete execution stays inconclusive.
